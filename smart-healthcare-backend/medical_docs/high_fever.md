# High Fever

A high fever is generally defined as a body temperature at or above
39°C (102.2°F). It is the body's natural response to infection or
inflammation. Common causes include bacterial and viral infections,
heatstroke, and, less commonly, inflammatory conditions or drug reactions.

High fever combined with a stiff neck, severe headache, confusion, a rash
that does not fade under pressure, or difficulty breathing requires urgent
evaluation, as these may indicate a serious infection such as meningitis
or sepsis. Fever alone, without these warning signs, is often manageable
with rest, fluids, and antipyretics, but persistent high fever (more than
2-3 days) should be assessed by a clinician.
