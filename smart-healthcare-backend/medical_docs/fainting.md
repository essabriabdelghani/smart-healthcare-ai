# Fainting (Syncope)

Fainting, or syncope, is a temporary loss of consciousness caused by a
sudden drop in blood flow to the brain. Common causes include vasovagal
reactions (triggered by stress, pain, or standing too long), dehydration,
low blood sugar, heart rhythm abnormalities, and a sudden drop in blood
pressure upon standing (orthostatic hypotension).

Fainting associated with chest pain, palpitations, exertion, or occurring
without warning signs is more concerning and may indicate an underlying
cardiac cause requiring prompt evaluation. Fainting preceded by
lightheadedness, sweating, and blurred vision in a hot or crowded
environment is typically less concerning but should still be assessed if
recurrent.
