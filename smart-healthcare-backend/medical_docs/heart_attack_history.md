# Heart Attack (Myocardial Infarction)

A heart attack occurs when blood flow to part of the heart muscle is
blocked, usually by a blood clot in a coronary artery, causing damage to
the heart tissue. A history of a prior heart attack is a significant risk
factor for future cardiovascular events and is an important element of a
patient's medical history when assessing new chest symptoms.

Typical warning signs include chest pain or pressure, pain radiating to
the arm/jaw/back, shortness of breath, cold sweats, and nausea. Anyone
with a history of heart attack presenting with new or recurrent chest
pain should be evaluated urgently, as their baseline cardiovascular risk
is elevated compared to the general population.
